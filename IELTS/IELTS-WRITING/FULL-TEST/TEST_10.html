Task type: Horizontal bar chart
Data pattern: Dynamic
Time period: 2009 and 2014
Unit: Number of homes valued at US$5 million or more
Suggested tense: Past simple

Task: The bar chart compares the numbers of homes worth at least US$5 million that were sold in eight cities in 2009 and 2014.

1. Task Analysis

ElementAnalysis
Visual typeA horizontal bar chart comparing two years.
Main subjectSales of homes valued at US$5 million or above.
CategoriesDubai, Singapore, Sydney, Los Angeles, Miami, Hong Kong, New York and London.
Time and unit2009 and 2014; number of homes sold.
Main comparisonChanges in luxury-home sales across the eight cities and differences between cities.
Grouping strategyBody 1: the four cities with the highest 2014 figures; Body 2: the remaining cities, including Singapore as the only decline.
Main cautionReport absolute numbers, not percentages; distinguish increases to a figure from increases by an amount.

2. Key Features

3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

Original subject: Numbers of very expensive homes sold in eight international cities.

Paraphrased introduction: The horizontal bar chart compares the number of properties valued at US$5 million or more that were sold in eight cities in 2009 and 2014.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, sales of high-value homes increased in all cities except Singapore. London recorded by far the highest figures and the most substantial growth, whereas Dubai remained the smallest market in both years.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, London, New York and Hong Kong dominated luxury-property sales, with London widening its lead considerably by 2014. Most of the lower-ranking cities also experienced moderate growth, while Singapore was the only city to register a decline.

Best choice: Option A
Reason: It clearly presents the overall trend, the main exception and both extremes.

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type: Mixed / Hybrid Essay: Reasons + Positive or Negative Development
Band target: 6.5–8.0
Topic area: Society / Migration / Lifestyle

Task: In the past, people lived in the same place throughout their lives. However, it is common that now people change their place and often live in different places during their life. What are the reasons for this? Is it a positive or negative development?

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeA mixed essay requiring reasons for geographical mobility and an evaluation of whether it is positive or negative.
Main topicThe increasing tendency for people to relocate several times during their lives.
Keywordschange their place, live in different places, reasons, positive or negative development.
Task focusExplain the main causes of frequent relocation and judge its overall impact on individuals and society.
Required positionA clear positive, negative or balanced judgment must be maintained throughout the essay.
Plain-English meaningWhy do people move more frequently nowadays, and is this change generally beneficial or harmful?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

General statement: Modern societies are considerably more mobile than communities in previous generations.

Paraphrased task: Rather than remaining in their hometowns permanently, many people now relocate repeatedly for professional, educational or personal purposes.

Thesis: This trend is mainly driven by the pursuit of better employment and education, as well as changing family and lifestyle needs; overall, it is a positive development because it broadens opportunities and encourages adaptability, despite occasionally weakening community ties.

Body Paragraph 1 — Reasons for Frequent Relocation

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentencePeople primarily move to access better opportunities and respond to changing personal circumstances.
Expl1Explanation 1High-quality universities and specialised jobs are often concentrated in major cities or economically developed regions.
ExampleConcrete exampleA graduate from a small town may relocate to a capital city to obtain professional training and a well-paid position.
Expl2Explanation 2People may later move again because of marriage, housing costs, family responsibilities or a preference for a different lifestyle.
Further dev.DepthImproved transport, remote communication and greater international connectivity have also made relocation less expensive and socially disruptive than before.
LinkParagraph linkFrequent movement is therefore a practical response to both economic opportunities and different stages of adult life.

Body Paragraph 2 — Overall Evaluation: Largely Positive

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceAlthough repeated relocation can create instability, its long-term benefits are generally more substantial.
Expl1Explanation 1Mobility enables people to find places that better match their qualifications, ambitions and financial needs.
ExampleConcrete exampleAn employee who moves to a region with a stronger industry may gain advanced skills, higher earnings and better career prospects.
Expl2Explanation 2Living in different communities can also develop independence, cultural awareness and the ability to adapt to unfamiliar environments.
Further dev.DepthAdmittedly, moving may weaken friendships and a sense of belonging; however, modern communication and deliberate community involvement can reduce these effects, whereas improved education and employment may influence a person’s entire future.
LinkParagraph linkConsequently, the lasting gains in opportunity and personal development outweigh the usually manageable social disadvantages.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

Restated thesis: People relocate more frequently because employment, education and personal circumstances increasingly require geographical flexibility.

Summary: While moving can reduce community stability and disrupt relationships, it provides greater access to opportunities and promotes personal adaptability.

Final thought: It should therefore be regarded as a predominantly positive development.
