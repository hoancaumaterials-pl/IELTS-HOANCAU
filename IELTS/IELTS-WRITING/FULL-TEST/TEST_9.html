Task: The charts compare the average retirement ages of men and women in seven countries in 2004 and 2008.

3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

Original subject: Average retirement ages of men and women in seven countries in 2004 and 2008.

Paraphrased introduction: The two bar charts compare the average ages at which males and females retired in seven countries in 2004 and 2008.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, Sweden consistently recorded the highest retirement ages for both genders, whereas France had the lowest figures. Most countries experienced increases between 2004 and 2008, although the age for Italian men was unchanged and those for women in France and Italy fell slightly.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, men generally retired later than women, particularly in Denmark and Germany. Sweden ranked first for both sexes, while retirement ages in France remained at approximately 59 years.

Best choice: Option A
Reason: It covers the rankings, overall movement and major exceptions.

Body Paragraph 1

ComponentOutline
GroupSweden, the Netherlands, the UK and Denmark.
Opening comparisonThese countries generally recorded the highest retirement ages.
Detail 1Sweden led both charts, with men rising from roughly 63.5 to nearly 64 and women from just over 63 to around 63.5.
Detail 2In the Netherlands, male retirement age remained close to 63, while the female figure increased from 62 to approximately 62.5.
Detail 3Denmark and the UK also saw increases; men retired at around 62–63, compared with approximately 60–62 for women.
Useful structureWhile men in X retired at around…, the corresponding figure for women was approximately….

Body Paragraph 2

ComponentOutline
GroupFrance, Germany and Italy.
Opening comparisonThese countries recorded lower figures and included the main exceptions.
Detail 1France had the lowest ages, at about 59 for both sexes; the male figure rose marginally, while the female figure declined slightly.
Detail 2Germany saw noticeable increases, from around 61 to just over 62 for men and from approximately 60.5 to above 61 for women.
Detail 3Italy’s male figure remained stable at 60, whereas the female average fell slightly from 60 to just under this level.
Useful structureBy contrast, X remained unchanged at…, whereas Y decreased marginally to….

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type: Opinion / Agree or Disagree
Band target: 6.5–8.0
Topic area: Tourism, Culture and Environment

Task: In recent years, tourists have paid attention to preserving both the culture and environment of the places they visit. However, some people think that it is impossible to be a responsible tourist. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeAn opinion essay requiring the writer to state how strongly they agree or disagree.
Main topicWhether tourists can behave responsibly towards host communities and natural environments.
Keywordstourists, preserving culture, environment, impossible, responsible tourist, agree or disagree
Task focusEvaluate whether responsible tourism is realistically achievable rather than merely describing its benefits.
Required positionA clear and consistent degree of agreement or disagreement must be maintained throughout.
Plain-English meaningCan tourists genuinely protect local cultures and environments while travelling?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

General statement: International tourism can benefit destinations, but it can also place pressure on local traditions and ecosystems.

Paraphrased task: Although travellers are becoming more conscious of these effects, some argue that avoiding harmful behaviour while travelling is impossible.

Thesis: I strongly disagree because tourists can protect cultural heritage through respectful conduct and local spending, while practical environmental choices can substantially reduce the damage associated with travel.

Body Paragraph 1 — Cultural responsibility is achievable

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceTourists can behave responsibly by respecting local customs and ensuring that host communities benefit economically.
Expl1Explanation 1Learning basic social rules, dressing appropriately and requesting permission before photographing people can prevent cultural offence.
ExampleConcrete exampleA visitor to a religious site can follow its dress code, remain quiet and employ a knowledgeable local guide.
Expl2Explanation 2Travellers can also choose locally owned accommodation, restaurants and craft businesses instead of international chains.
Further dev.DepthThis directs tourism income towards residents and gives communities a financial reason to preserve traditional skills, festivals and historic places.
LinkParagraph linkResponsible cultural tourism is therefore possible when visitors prioritise respect and community participation over personal convenience.

Body Paragraph 2 — Environmental harm can be meaningfully reduced

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceAlthough no journey is completely impact-free, tourists can greatly limit avoidable environmental damage.
Expl1Explanation 1They can use public transport, carry reusable containers, conserve water and avoid activities that exploit wildlife.
ExampleConcrete exampleOn an island holiday, a traveller might use refill stations and select a small-group tour that follows established walking routes.
Expl2Explanation 2Choosing environmentally responsible accommodation and conservation-based activities can also encourage tourism businesses to adopt sustainable practices.
Further dev.DepthAdmittedly, flights and accommodation still consume resources; however, responsible tourism means reducing preventable harm rather than achieving an unrealistic state of zero impact.
LinkParagraph linkSince individual choices can produce significant and lasting improvements, environmental responsibility is difficult but certainly not impossible.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

Restated thesis: I strongly disagree that responsible tourism is impossible.

Summary: Although travel inevitably creates some cultural and environmental pressure, visitors can reduce preventable harm by respecting local norms, supporting community businesses and adopting lower-impact habits.

Final thought: Responsibility should be measured by meaningful harm reduction, not perfect neutrality.
