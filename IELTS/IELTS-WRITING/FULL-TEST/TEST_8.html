TASK 1 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Task type: Three maps showing changes to a geographical area
Data pattern: Map comparison
Time period: 1800–2000
Unit: Not stated
Suggested tense: Past simple with passive structures
Task: The maps illustrate how Brindell developed over the two-hundred-year period from 1800 to 2000.

1. Task Analysis

ElementAnalysis
Visual typeThree historical maps of the same area.
Main subjectChanges in Brindell’s land use and development.
CategoriesFarmland, woodland, the River Stour, Brindell, Bun Hill, workers’ housing, industry and parkland.
Time and unit1800, 1900 and 2000; no measurement unit.
Main comparisonAgricultural land was progressively replaced by residential, industrial and recreational developments.
Grouping strategyBody 1: changes from 1800 to 1900; Body 2: further development from 1900 to 2000.
Main cautionDo not claim that the river or settlement was relocated; both remained in broadly the same positions.

2. Key Features

PriorityKey feature
1Brindell changed from a predominantly agricultural area into a more developed industrial and residential location.
2Crop farming remained in the north-west until 1900 but had been replaced by animal farming by 2000.
3Animal farmland in the south-east disappeared as Bun Hill, workers’ housing and industry were established.
4The industrial area expanded considerably between 1900 and 2000.
5Woodland contracted but remained in the north-east, while the River Stour and Brindell stayed broadly unchanged.
6A park was added around Bun Hill by 2000.

3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

  • Original subject: The development of Brindell between 1800 and 2000.
  • Paraphrased introduction: The three maps illustrate the transformation of Brindell over a period of two centuries, from 1800 to 2000.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, Brindell became increasingly urbanised, as substantial areas of farmland were replaced by industrial, residential and recreational facilities. Meanwhile, the River Stour and the original settlement remained largely unchanged, although the woodland area became smaller.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, agriculture dominated Brindell in 1800, whereas industry and workers’ accommodation had become prominent features by 2000. The most notable changes occurred around Bun Hill and to the east of the river.

Best choice: Option A
Reason: It summarises both the dominant transformation and the main unchanged features.

Detail 2Woodland covered the north-east, and Brindell was a small settlement in the south-west.
Detail 3By 1900, the south-eastern animal farmland had been replaced by Bun Hill, a village for workers and an industrial area further east.

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type: Causes and Solutions
Band target: 6.5–8.0
Topic area: Environment / Waste management
Task: In some countries, there is not enough recycling of waste materials (e.g. paper, glass, and cans). What are the reasons and solutions?

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeA causes-and-solutions essay requiring one paragraph on causes and one on corresponding measures.
Main topicInsufficient recycling of common household waste in certain countries.
Keywordsnot enough recycling, waste materials, reasons, solutions, paper, glass, cans
Task focusExplain why recycling rates remain low and propose practical ways to increase them.
Required positionNo personal opinion is required, but the main causes and most effective solutions must be clearly identified.
Plain-English meaningWhy do people and organisations recycle too little, and how can this problem be addressed?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

  • General statement: Recycling is essential for reducing landfill use, conserving raw materials and limiting environmental damage.
  • Paraphrased task: Nevertheless, many countries still fail to recycle sufficient quantities of everyday materials such as paper, glass and metal containers.
Thesis: This problem is mainly caused by inadequate recycling infrastructure and weak public motivation, while more accessible collection systems, stronger financial incentives and sustained environmental education could substantially improve recycling rates.

Body Paragraph 1 — Reasons

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceLow recycling rates largely result from inconvenient waste-management systems and insufficient incentives for individuals and businesses.
Expl1Explanation 1In many communities, separate recycling bins, collection services and processing facilities are either unavailable or difficult to access.
ExampleConcrete exampleA household with only one general-waste bin is likely to mix paper, bottles and cans with food waste rather than transport them elsewhere.
Expl2Explanation 2Recycling may also appear time-consuming or financially unrewarding, particularly when disposable products are cheap and no penalties apply to unnecessary waste.
Further dev.DepthLimited public understanding compounds the issue because residents may not know which materials are recyclable or how incorrect sorting contaminates an entire batch.
LinkParagraph linkTherefore, both structural inconvenience and weak motivation discourage consistent recycling behaviour.

Body Paragraph 2 — Solutions

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceGovernments should combine convenient recycling services with economic and educational measures that directly address these barriers.
Expl1Explanation 1Local authorities should provide clearly labelled household bins, reliable kerbside collection and sufficient sorting and processing facilities.
ExampleConcrete exampleA deposit-return scheme could allow consumers to recover a small payment whenever they return glass bottles or metal cans.
Expl2Explanation 2Charges for excessive general waste, alongside rewards for correctly separated materials, would make recycling financially worthwhile.
Further dev.DepthSchools, media campaigns and product labels should explain correct sorting, while manufacturers could be required to use recyclable packaging and help fund its collection.
LinkParagraph linkBy making recycling both convenient and economically attractive, these policies would create lasting changes in public and corporate behaviour.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

  • Restated thesis: Insufficient recycling is primarily linked to poor infrastructure, limited awareness and a lack of meaningful incentives.
  • Summary: Expanding collection facilities, introducing financial rewards and responsibilities, and improving public education would address these causes directly.
  • Final thought: Coordinated action by governments, producers and households is therefore essential.
