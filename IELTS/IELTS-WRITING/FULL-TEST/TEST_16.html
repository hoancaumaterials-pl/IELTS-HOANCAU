PART A — IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1

Task type: Maps / plans showing change after a natural event

Data pattern: Map comparison

Time period: Before and after a volcanic eruption; exact dates not stated

Unit: Not applicable

Suggested tense: Past simple, with passive structures for destruction and survival

Task: The pictures illustrate the position of a volcano, and a village before and after a volcanic eruption. Provide an overview of the information by identifying and describing the key details, and include comparisons where appropriate.

A1. Task Analysis

ElementAnalysis
Visual typeThree diagrams: one locates the volcano, while the other two compare the village before and after the eruption.
Main subjectThe physical impact of a volcanic eruption on settlements, ponds and palm trees on the island.
Categories/featuresVolcano, lava-affected land, two residential clusters, two ponds, palm trees and the coastline.
Time and unitBefore and after the eruption; no dates or numerical unit are provided.
Main reporting focusThe direction and extent of the lava flow, the destruction of the left-hand settlement and the relative survival of the right-hand area.
Grouping strategyBody 1: original village layout. Body 2: changes caused by the eruption and features that remained.
Main accuracy riskAvoid claiming that every building was destroyed: several houses survived between the lava field and the right-hand settlement.

A2. Key Features

PriorityKey feature

4Several palm trees close to the volcano and within the affected zone were lost, although trees survived farther to the right.
5The coastline and the position of the volcano did not change.

A5. Model Report — Band 7.5+

The diagrams show the location of a volcano on an island and compare the nearby village before and after its eruption.

Overall, the eruption caused the left and central sections to undergo substantial transformation, as much of this area was covered by lava and most of the nearer settlement disappeared. By contrast, the residential area on the right remained largely intact.

Before the eruption, the volcano was situated at the far end of the island on the left. Two groups of houses occupied the remaining land: one in the centre and another on the right. In each case, the buildings were arranged around a pond, while palm trees were scattered across the area, including several near the volcano.

Afterwards, a lava field extended from the volcano towards the centre. The pond in the nearer settlement, together with most surrounding houses and palms, was completely removed. A few homes and trees nevertheless survived the eruption beyond the affected zone. In comparison, the right-hand settlement retained its original layout: its pond, surrounding houses and several palm trees were still present. The coastline also showed no noticeable change.

PART B — IELTS WRITING TASK 2

Essay type: Two-Part / Double-Question Essay

Band target: 7.5+

Topic area: Society / Employment / Lifestyle

Task: In the past, people lived in one place for a long time, but now they can live in many different places. What are the reasons? Is this a positive or negative development?

B1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeTwo-part question. Body 1 should explain the reasons; Body 2 should evaluate whether the change is positive or negative.
Main topicThe growing tendency for people to live in several places during their lifetime.
Keywordsin the past; lived in one place; now; many different places; reasons; positive or negative development
Task focusExplain why residential mobility has increased and give a clear overall judgement about its effects.
Required positionYes. The writer must decide whether the development is mainly positive or mainly negative.
Plain-English meaningWhy do people relocate more often today, and is this change beneficial overall?
Main trapDo not discuss travel or tourism only; the focus is changing one’s place of residence.

B2. Writing Outline

Introduction — target 45–60 words

Opening context: Remaining in one community for most of one’s life is less common than it once was.

Paraphrased task: Modern adults increasingly move between cities or countries rather than settling permanently in a single place.

Thesis: This shift is mainly driven by easier transport, digital connectivity and the pursuit of work or education, and I consider it broadly positive because it expands personal choice and opportunity despite some loss of stability.

Body Paragraph 1 — Reasons for increased residential mobility

PartFunctionSentence/idea
TSTopic sentencePeople can relocate more frequently because both movement and work have become less tied to one locality.
Expl1ExplanationFaster, cheaper transport and online communication make distant places easier to reach and relationships easier to maintain.
ExampleConcrete exampleA graduate can move to another city for a first job while staying in regular contact with family through video calls.
Expl2Further explanationEmployment markets and universities encourage mobility because specialised opportunities are concentrated in particular regions or countries.
Further developmentDepthRemote and hybrid work also allow some employees to change residence without changing employer.
LinkParagraph linkTogether, these changes have reduced both the practical and emotional cost of relocation.

Body Paragraph 2 — Mainly positive development

PartFunctionSentence/idea
TSTopic sentenceOverall, greater mobility is beneficial because it gives people more control over where they study, work and build their lives.
Expl1ExplanationMoving can provide access to better-paid employment, stronger educational programmes and safer or more suitable living conditions.
ExampleConcrete exampleA worker from a small town may relocate to a larger city for professional training and later choose a quieter area when raising a family.
Expl2Further explanationLiving in different communities can broaden perspectives, develop independence and strengthen cultural adaptability.
Further developmentDepthFrequent moves may weaken local ties or create rootlessness, but these costs can be reduced when relocation is voluntary and social connections are maintained.
LinkParagraph linkThe gains in autonomy and life chances therefore outweigh the disadvantages of reduced permanence.

Conclusion — target 35–50 words

Restated position: The rise in residential mobility is chiefly a positive change.

Summary: Technology, transport and mobile careers have made relocation easier, while moving enables people to pursue opportunities and select environments that suit different life stages.

Final implication: Its drawbacks are real but generally manageable when people retain meaningful social connections.

B4. Model Essay — Band 7.5+

Living in one community for most of one’s life is less common than it once was. Modern adults increasingly move between cities or countries rather than settling permanently. This shift stems mainly from improved transport, digital connectivity and mobile careers, and I regard it as positive because it expands choice and opportunity.

Several factors explain the rise in residential mobility. First, affordable long-distance travel makes distant places easier to reach, while video calls help people maintain relationships after moving. A graduate can therefore accept a job in another city without losing close contact with family. Second, people relocate to pursue employment opportunities or access higher education unavailable locally. Remote and hybrid work has also weakened the link between employment and a fixed address, allowing some workers to relocate with relative ease while keeping the same job. These changes have reduced the practical and emotional costs of moving.

In my view, this is mainly beneficial because it creates greater personal autonomy. People can choose locations that suit changing priorities, perhaps moving to a city for training and later to a quieter area to raise children. Exposure to different communities can broaden one’s horizons and develop cultural adaptability, both valuable in diverse workplaces. Admittedly, repeated moves may weaken long-standing community ties and produce a sense of rootlessness. However, these costs are less serious when relocation is voluntary and people maintain family and friendships. The long-term gains in education, employment and freedom therefore outweigh reduced permanence.

In conclusion, better transport, communication technology and flexible work explain why people now live in several places. Although mobility may reduce stability, it is positive overall because it enables individuals to pursue opportunities and choose environments suited to different life stages.
