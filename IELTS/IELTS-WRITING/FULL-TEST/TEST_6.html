3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

  • Original subject: The percentages of Australian children in three age groups participating in four leisure activities in 2012.
  • Paraphrased introduction: The grouped bar chart compares the proportions of Australian children aged 5–8, 9–11 and 12–16 who took part in four recreational activities in 2012.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, watching television was by far the most common activity among children of all ages, whereas art attracted the smallest proportions. Participation in art generally decreased with age, while the oldest group recorded the highest figure for bike riding.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, the oldest children were the most likely to watch television and ride a bike, while the youngest group showed the greatest interest in art. By contrast, reading participation remained relatively similar across all three age categories.

Best choice: Option A
Reason: It highlights both the overall ranking of activities and the clearest age-related patterns.

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type: Opinion / Agree or Disagree
Band target: 6.5–8.0
Topic area: Technology and public spending
Task: Some people think the money spent on developing technology for space exploration is wasteful. There are more beneficial ways to spend this money. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeAn opinion essay requiring a clear judgment about whether investment in space technology is wasteful.
Main topicThe allocation of public money between space exploration and urgent needs on Earth.
Keywordsmoney spent, developing technology, space exploration, wasteful, more beneficial ways, agree or disagree
Task focusEvaluate the value of space-related investment and compare it with alternative areas of public expenditure.
Required positionA clear degree of agreement must be maintained; this outline largely agrees while recognising limited benefits.
Plain-English meaningShould governments spend less on space technology and use the money to solve problems on Earth instead?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

  • General statement: Governments must decide how to distribute limited financial resources among competing national priorities.
  • Paraphrased task: While some regard expenditure on space technology as unnecessary, others believe that it produces scientific and practical benefits.
  • Position: Investment in space research has some value, but urgent social and environmental needs should receive greater financial priority.
Thesis: I largely agree that excessive spending on space exploration is difficult to justify because the same resources could improve essential public services and address immediate environmental challenges, although a limited level of space research should be maintained.

Body Paragraph 1 — More urgent social priorities

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentencePublic money can generate more immediate and widespread benefits when allocated to essential services.
Expl1Explanation 1Many communities lack adequate healthcare, affordable housing and high-quality education, all of which directly affect living standards.
ExampleConcrete exampleFunding used for a single costly space project could instead equip hospitals, train teachers or expand clean-water systems in disadvantaged regions.
Expl2Explanation 2These investments benefit a much larger proportion of the population than highly specialised space missions.
Further dev.DepthBetter public services also strengthen workforce productivity and reduce inequality, producing long-term economic as well as social returns.
LinkParagraph linkTherefore, where basic needs remain unmet, prioritising space exploration represents a significant opportunity cost.

Body Paragraph 2 — Environmental priorities and limited value of space research

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceEnvironmental protection is another stronger priority, although space technology should not be abandoned entirely.
Expl1Explanation 1Governments require substantial funding to develop renewable energy, protect ecosystems and improve resilience to floods, droughts and extreme heat.
ExampleConcrete exampleInvestment in cleaner public transport and renewable electricity could reduce urban pollution while lowering dependence on fossil fuels.
Expl2Explanation 2Admittedly, satellites support weather forecasting, global communication and the monitoring of environmental change.
Further dev.DepthHowever, these practical applications can be funded selectively, whereas expensive missions with uncertain benefits should receive less support than measures addressing present and highly probable threats.
LinkParagraph linkThus, the scale and urgency of terrestrial problems make them more deserving of public expenditure than unrestricted space development.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

  • Restated thesis: Overall, I agree to a large extent that spending on space technology should be reduced.
  • Summary: Healthcare, education and environmental protection offer more direct, extensive and immediate benefits.
  • Final thought: Nevertheless, governments should preserve carefully targeted space programmes that provide clear practical value to society.
