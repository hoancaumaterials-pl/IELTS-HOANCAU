Data pattern: Dynamic

Time period: 1972, 1992 and 2012

Unit: Percentage of total book sales

Suggested tense: Past simple

Task: The pie charts below show the percentage of five kinds of books sold by a bookseller between 1972 and 2012. Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

A3. Writing Outline

Introduction — one sentence

Original subject: Percentages of five book types sold by one bookseller in three different years.

Paraphrased introduction: The three pie charts compare the proportions of a bookseller’s sales accounted for by five book categories in 1972, 1992 and 2012.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended: Overall, fiction became increasingly dominant, especially adult fiction, which represented almost half of all sales by 2012. By contrast, biography, travel and other books all lost market share over the period.

Option B — Alternative: Overall, “others” was the largest category at the beginning, but adult fiction moved into first place and widened its lead substantially. Meanwhile, biography became the smallest segment by the final year.

Best choice: Option A

Reason: It captures both the central overall trend and the contrast between fiction and the remaining categories.

A5. Model Report — Band 7.5+

The three pie charts compare the proportions of a bookseller’s sales accounted for by five book categories in 1972, 1992 and 2012.

Overall, fiction became increasingly dominant, especially adult fiction, which grew to represent almost half of total sales by 2012. By contrast, biography, travel and other books all lost market share across the period.

In 1972, adult fiction, children’s fiction and biography each accounted for 20% of sales. Over the next two decades, adult fiction rose by 5 percentage points and became the largest category, while children’s fiction edged up to 22%. By 2012, the former had climbed sharply to 45%, almost double its 1992 share, whereas the latter reached 25%. Together, the two fiction categories made up 70% of sales, compared with only 40% in 1972.

The remaining categories followed an overall downward pattern. Other books, initially the leading segment at 25%, fell steadily to 20% in 1992 and then shrank to 12% in 2012. Travel showed a temporary increase from 15% to 18% before declining to 10%. Biography recorded the steepest proportional fall, dropping from 20% to 15% and finally to just 8%, the smallest share in the final year.

Essay type: Opinion / Agree or Disagree

Band target: 7.5+

Topic area: Environment / Government

Task: Solving environmental problems should be the responsibility of an international organization rather than each national government. Do you agree or disagree?

B1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeOpinion essay. State a clear level of agreement and defend it consistently.
Main topicWho should hold primary responsibility for addressing environmental problems.
Keywordssolving environmental problems; responsibility; international organisation; rather than; each national government; agree or disagree
Task focusEvaluate whether responsibility should be transferred from national governments to an international body.
Required positionYes. A direct and consistent opinion is required.
Plain-English meaningShould a global organisation, instead of individual governments, be mainly responsible for environmental protection?
Main trapDo not discuss international cooperation generally without deciding whether it should replace national responsibility.

B2. Writing Outline

Introduction — target 45–60 words

Opening context: Many environmental threats cross borders and therefore require cooperation between countries.

Paraphrased task: Some people argue that a single international organisation should take responsibility for solving these problems instead of separate national authorities.

Thesis: Although international organisations should coordinate common standards, expertise and finance, I disagree that they should replace national governments, because effective action ultimately depends on domestic law, local implementation and public accountability.

Restated position: International bodies should coordinate rather than replace national authorities.

Summary: They are best placed to set shared goals and distribute expertise or funding, while governments must enact and enforce context-specific measures.

B4. Model Essay — Band 7.5+

Environmental damage frequently crosses national borders, making international cooperation indispensable. Although global institutions should coordinate common goals and resources, I disagree that they ought to assume responsibility instead of national governments. The most workable arrangement is a global framework backed by strong domestic implementation.

International organisations should play a leading coordinating role because climate change, ocean pollution and biodiversity loss are transboundary environmental threats. Action by one country can be undermined if others do little. Global bodies can establish a binding international framework, harmonise environmental standards and pool scientific expertise, enabling less-developed states to benefit from wider technical knowledge. They can also mobilise climate finance for projects that individual governments could not afford. For example, a regional organisation could coordinate river-basin rules so that upstream industrial activity does not impose costs on downstream communities. Without such collective action, governments may pursue short-term national interests and neglect problems whose benefits are shared globally.

Nevertheless, national governments must retain primary responsibility because they possess the legal authority and local knowledge needed for implementation. It is national governments that can tax polluters, regulate factories, protect forests and prosecute environmental offences. They can also tailor policies to local conditions: a densely populated city may need low-emission zones, whereas an agricultural region may require controls on fertiliser use. Moreover, elected governments provide democratic accountability, while international bodies often lack both enforcement powers and public legitimacy. Even an excellent treaty has little value unless domestic enforcement mechanisms translate it into inspections, budgets and penalties. International cooperation is therefore most effective when it supports states rather than attempts to replace them.

In conclusion, international organisations are vital for coordination, finance and common standards, but environmental protection cannot be transferred away from national authorities. Global institutions should define shared objectives, while governments remain accountable for delivering them through enforceable domestic policy.
