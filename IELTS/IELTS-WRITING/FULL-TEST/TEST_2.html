Task: Summarise and compare the percentages of male and female driving-licence holders in one European country, including figures for drivers aged 17–20.

1. Task Analysis

ElementAnalysis
Visual typeTwo bar charts showing changes over time.
Main subjectDriving-licence ownership among men, women and young drivers.
CategoriesMen and women overall; male and female drivers aged 17–20.
Time and unit1976–2006; percentages.
Main comparisonGender differences and contrasting trends between the overall population and young drivers.
Grouping strategyBody 1: total licence holders; Body 2: drivers aged 17–20.
Main cautionUse percentage points, not percentages, when describing differences between figures.

2. Key Features

PriorityKey featureSupporting data
1Licence ownership increased overall, especially among women.Men: 70% to 80%; women: 30% to 60%.
2Men consistently recorded higher rates than women.The male figure remained above the female figure in every year.
3The overall gender gap narrowed considerably.From 40 percentage points in 1976 to 20 points in 2006.
4The trend among young drivers was the opposite.Male rate fell from 50% to 44%; female rate from 28% to 19%.
5Young women experienced the larger absolute decline.Down 9 points, compared with 6 points for young men.
6Young-driver rates were substantially below the corresponding total figures.In 2006: 44% versus 80% for men; 19% versus 60% for women.

3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

Original subject: The percentages of male and female driving-licence holders overall and among 17–20-year-olds.

Paraphrased introduction: The two bar charts compare the proportions of men and women holding driving licences in a European country between 1976 and 2006, with additional data for drivers aged 17–20 in 1996 and 2006.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, men were consistently more likely than women to possess a driving licence. While licence ownership rose among both sexes in the population as a whole, it declined among drivers aged 17–20.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, women recorded the greatest increase in total licence ownership, causing the gender gap to narrow substantially. By contrast, the proportions of young male and female drivers both fell between 1996 and 2006.

Best choice: Option A

Reason: It highlights the dominant gender difference and the clearest contrast between the two charts.

Body Paragraph 1

ComponentOutline
GroupTotal male and female licence holders, 1976–2006.
Opening comparisonBoth figures increased, but growth among women was much stronger.
Detail 1Men remained stable at 70% from 1976 to 1986, then rose gradually to 80% in 2006.
Detail 2Women increased continuously from 30% to 60%, doubling over the period.
Detail 3The gender difference narrowed from 40 to 20 percentage points.
Useful structureWhereas the male rate rose modestly from X to Y, the corresponding female figure increased considerably from A to B.

Body Paragraph 2

ComponentOutline
GroupMale and female drivers aged 17–20 in 1996 and 2006.
Opening comparisonUnlike the overall population, both young-driver groups experienced declines.
Detail 1Young men fell from 50% in 1996 to 44% in 2006.
Detail 2Young women declined more markedly, from 28% to 19%.
Detail 3In 2006, both figures were far below the total rates of 80% and 60% respectively.
Useful structureIn contrast to the upward overall trend, the proportion of young X fell from…, while that of young Y dropped from…

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type
Discuss Both Views and Give Your Opinion
Band target
6.5–8.0
Topic area
Child development / Family and society
Task: Some people think that family has the most powerful influence on a child’s development, while others believe that other factors (friends, television, music,...) have a stronger effect. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeDiscuss both perspectives fairly, then present and maintain a clear personal opinion.
Main topicThe relative influence of family and external social factors on children’s development.
Keywordsfamily, most powerful influence, child’s development, friends, television, music, stronger effect
Task focusExplain why families may be dominant and why peers or media may exert greater influence.
Required positionA direct judgment is required about which influence is ultimately stronger.
Plain-English meaningDo families or outside influences shape children more significantly, and why?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

  • General statement: Children’s personalities, values and behaviour are shaped by several influences throughout their formative years.
  • Paraphrased task: While some argue that parents and relatives play the decisive role, others believe that friends and popular media have a greater impact.
Thesis: Although peers and media can strongly affect children’s preferences and behaviour, I believe that family remains the most influential force because it provides their earliest moral framework and continues to guide how they respond to outside pressures.

Body Paragraph 1 — Family as the Primary Influence

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceSupporters of the first view argue that family has the deepest influence because children begin learning attitudes and behaviour at home.
Expl1Explanation 1Parents model communication, discipline, responsibility and emotional responses during the earliest and most impressionable stage of life.
ExampleConcrete exampleA child whose parents consistently show respect and resolve disagreements calmly is likely to adopt similar behaviour.
Expl2Explanation 2Families also make major decisions concerning education, routines, friendships and access to digital content.
Further dev.DepthThese early values often become an internal standard through which children later judge messages received from friends, television or music.
LinkParagraph linkFamily influence is therefore both foundational and relatively long-lasting.

Body Paragraph 2 — External Factors and the Writer’s Position

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceNevertheless, friends and media can become highly influential as children grow older and seek independence from their parents.
Expl1Explanation 1Peer acceptance may affect their language, clothing, interests and willingness to take risks, particularly during adolescence.
ExampleConcrete exampleA teenager may begin following a musical trend or adopting certain habits simply because these are popular within their friendship group.
Expl2Explanation 2Television and online entertainment repeatedly expose young people to lifestyles and values that may not be taught at home.
Further dev.DepthHowever, the effect of these influences often depends on the judgment and self-control already developed within the family; supportive parenting can help children question harmful social pressure.
LinkParagraph linkExternal forces are powerful, but family usually determines whether children accept, modify or reject them.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

  • Restated thesis: In conclusion, both family and external influences contribute substantially to a child’s development.
  • Summary: Friends and media become increasingly important with age, but families establish children’s earliest values and shape their reactions to later influences.
  • Final thought: For this reason, family generally remains the more powerful force.
