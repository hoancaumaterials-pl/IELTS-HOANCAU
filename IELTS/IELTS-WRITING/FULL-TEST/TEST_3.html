3. Writing Skeleton

Introduction — one sentence

Original subject: The percentages of men, women and children in the UK consuming the recommended daily amount of fruit and vegetables.

Paraphrased introduction: The bar chart illustrates the proportions of British men, women and children who ate the recommended daily quantity of fruit and vegetables in 2002, 2006 and 2010.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, the proportions for all three groups rose between 2002 and 2006 before falling in 2010. Women consistently had the highest figures, whereas children recorded the lowest percentages throughout.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, consumption reached a peak among every group in 2006, with women showing the highest rate at each point. Although all categories declined afterwards, their 2010 figures remained above the corresponding levels in 2002.

Best choice: Option A
Reason: It presents both the common trend and the clearest contrast between the groups.

Body Paragraph 1

ComponentOutline
GroupMen and women
Opening comparisonWomen had higher proportions than men in every year, although both followed the same general trend.
Detail 1Men increased from 22% in 2002 to 26% in 2006.
Detail 2Women rose more markedly, from 25% to the chart’s peak of 32%.
Detail 3By 2010, the figures had fallen to 24% for men and 26% for women.
Useful structureWhereas the proportion of X rose from … to …, the figure for Y increased from … to … before both declined.

Body Paragraph 2

ComponentOutline
GroupChildren and comparisons with adults
Opening comparisonChildren consistently recorded substantially lower percentages than both adult groups.
Detail 1Their figure climbed from only 11% in 2002 to 18% in 2006.
Detail 2It then decreased slightly to 16% in 2010, remaining 8 points below men and 10 below women.
Detail 3The adult–child gap narrowed by the end of the period, particularly between women and children.
Useful structureAlthough X remained the smallest group, its proportion increased from … to … before slipping to ….

TASK 2 – OUTLINE THAM KHẢO

Essay type
Opinion / Agree or Disagree
Band target
6.5–8.0
Topic area
Education and government funding
Task: Some people believe that everyone has a right to have access to university education and the governments should make it free for all students no matter what financial background they have. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

1. Task Analysis

ElementDetail
Essay typeAn opinion essay requiring a clear judgment on whether university should be free for every student.
Main topicUniversal access to publicly funded higher education.
Keywordseveryone, right, access, university education, governments, free, all students, financial background.
Task focusEvaluate both educational equality and the financial practicality of abolishing tuition fees universally.
Required positionA clear degree of agreement or disagreement must be maintained throughout the essay.
Plain-English meaningShould governments pay the university tuition fees of all students, including those from wealthy families?

2. Skeleton

Introduction — 50–70 words

General statement: University education can improve individuals’ employment prospects and contribute to a country’s economic and social development.

Paraphrased task: It is therefore argued that higher education should be treated as a universal right and provided without tuition fees, regardless of students’ financial circumstances.

Thesis: I largely disagree with universal free university education because it would place an excessive burden on public finances and subsidise families who can already afford tuition; however, governments should fully support capable students from disadvantaged backgrounds.

Body Paragraph 1 — Drawbacks of universal free tuition

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceMaking university free for every student would be financially inefficient and could divert public money from more urgent services.
Expl1Explanation 1Governments would have to fund tuition for millions of students while continuing to finance schools, hospitals, transport and social welfare.
ExampleConcrete exampleA government with a limited education budget might have to postpone the construction of primary schools in rural communities to cover university costs.
Expl2Explanation 2Universal funding would also benefit students from affluent households who are already able to pay their own fees.
Further dev.DepthThis could make the policy regressive because taxpayers with modest incomes would help subsidise degrees for wealthier families.
LinkParagraph linkPublic funding should therefore be distributed according to financial need rather than granted automatically to everyone.

Body Paragraph 2 — Targeted support as a fairer alternative

PartFunctionSentence / Idea
TSTopic sentenceNevertheless, financial hardship should never prevent academically capable students from entering higher education.
Expl1Explanation 1Students from low-income families may avoid university because of tuition fees, accommodation costs and the need to earn money immediately.
ExampleConcrete exampleA talented student from a disadvantaged household might reject a university place because their family cannot afford either fees or living expenses.
Expl2Explanation 2Governments can address this inequality through means-tested scholarships, maintenance grants and affordable income-based loans.
Further dev.DepthUnlike universal free tuition, targeted assistance promotes social mobility while preserving public resources for other priorities and requiring wealthier students to contribute.
LinkParagraph linkThis approach protects equal opportunity without creating the unnecessary cost of financing every student regardless of need.

Conclusion — 40–55 words

Restated thesis: University should be accessible to all qualified students, but this does not mean that it must be entirely free for every individual.

Summary: Targeted financial support is fairer and more sustainable because it removes barriers for disadvantaged learners while avoiding unnecessary subsidies for affluent families.
