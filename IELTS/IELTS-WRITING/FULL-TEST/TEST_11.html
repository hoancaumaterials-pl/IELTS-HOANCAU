  • Paraphrased introduction: The diagrams compare the internal arrangement of the South Wing of Walton Museum in 2008 with its redesigned layout in 2012.

Overview — two sentences

Option A — Recommended

Overall, the South Wing was considerably enlarged and provided a much wider range of facilities by 2012. The main developments were the construction of a four-storey section, the replacement of the stairs with a lift and the addition of several visitor amenities.

Option B — Alternative

Overall, although the special exhibition room and shop remained on the lowest level, most other parts of the wing were reorganised or expanded. Particularly noticeable changes included the relocation of the cloakroom and the introduction of a restaurant, terrace and children’s play area.

Best choice: Option A

Reason: Cách này khái quát rõ nhất mức độ mở rộng và ba thay đổi cấu trúc quan trọng nhất.

A5. Model Report — Band 7.5+

The diagrams compare the South Wing of Walton Museum in 2008 with its layout after redevelopment in 2012.

Overall, the wing underwent substantial redevelopment, becoming larger and offering more visitor facilities. While the special exhibition room and shop were retained on the lowest level, the stairs were replaced by a lift, and a new four-storey section was created on the right.

In 2008, the lower level contained the special exhibition room, shop and cloaks room from left to right. A staircase between the shop and cloaks room provided access to the upper floor, where the reception was directly above the shop and the reading room stood over the cloaks room. The entrance was on the left at upper-ground level.

By 2012, the former cloaks room had been converted into a children’s play area, while the reception and cloakroom functions were combined in a room above the shop. An entrance hall was added above the special exhibition room, with a terrace on the next level, while a restaurant occupied the space above the reception-cloaks room. The enlarged right-hand block contained a children’s play area, reading room, self-service room and exhibition room on successive floors, all connected by the new lift.

B4. Model Essay — Band 7.5+

A growing number of parents are choosing home-based education rather than enrolling their children in conventional schools. Although this arrangement can provide flexibility and personalised support, I believe its disadvantages are greater because most families cannot replace the academic expertise and social environment that schools provide.

The main advantage is that parents can tailor instruction to individual needs. Children who struggle with a subject can spend longer on it, while faster learners may progress without waiting for a class. They may also follow a flexible learning schedule, which is useful for children with health conditions or families living far from suitable schools. In addition, one-to-one attention can help parents identify learning difficulties quickly. However, these benefits depend heavily on the time, knowledge and resources available within each household.

The more serious concern is that many parents lack specialised subject knowledge and pedagogical expertise. As children become older, teaching mathematics, science or foreign languages accurately becomes increasingly difficult. Unless parents possess the necessary expertise, children may develop gaps that restrict later study. Home education can also reduce social interaction with peers. Schools require pupils to cooperate, resolve disagreements and communicate with people from different backgrounds, helping them develop interpersonal skills. A child taught mainly at home may have fewer opportunities to practise these abilities, even when attending clubs. Moreover, full-time teaching can place a heavy burden on parents and widen educational inequality, since affluent families can hire tutors while others cannot. While flexibility is valuable, these academic and social risks have broader and longer-lasting consequences.

In conclusion, home education can offer personalised teaching and greater freedom, but these benefits do not outweigh the possible loss of professional instruction, everyday peer contact and equal educational opportunities.
